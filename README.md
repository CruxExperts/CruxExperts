## From the early web to AI

### 1995 — Building for the commercial web

I began developing websites commercially in 1995, in the era of **Netscape Navigator and early HTML**. As the web grew more interactive, I worked with **JavaScript and Perl**, then moved into **ASP and ASP.NET** as websites became full business applications.

My first experiences with hosting included **OpenBSD servers in university environments**. I was learning how to build for the web and how to keep the underlying servers running—an early connection between software development and infrastructure that has stayed with me throughout my career.

Alongside development, I built my skills in **search engine optimization before Google existed**. When Google arrived, earning top rankings for my own sites and for clients became a continuing fascination. I approached the web as both an engineer and a marketer: building the system, then understanding how people would find it.

### From around 2002 — Software that connected business and industrial equipment

As a software engineer at **Mazzella Lifting Technologies**, I developed corporate intranets and custom applications for industrial operations, including software for **load cells, force measurement, and industrial equipment**.

We built interfaces, reporting tools, and internal systems around the actual work. This gave me experience connecting software with physical processes, operational data, and the people responsible for them.

### Around 2005–2006 — Video processing and early YouTube

I was also fascinated by **video processing and editing**. Around 2005–2006, before Google's acquisition of YouTube, I registered an account and began creating and publishing videos on the young platform. It was another way to explore the connection between technology, content, and reaching an audience.

### iHost — From a bedroom to infrastructure across two data centers

I founded **iHost ASP / IHOST, LLC** in my bedroom, specializing first in ASP and then ASP.NET web hosting.

Over **nine years**, I grew the business to **more than 62,000 hosted domains and tens of thousands of users**, with full racks of colocated servers across **two data centers**, before selling the company.

For several years, iHost was an **official Microsoft partner**. We exhibited at **Microsoft trade shows in Las Vegas**, with our own booth, representing the hosting business we had built around Microsoft's web technologies.

Running that business meant taking responsibility for the whole operation: the technology, the infrastructure, the customers, and the decisions that kept it working. That experience still shapes how I approach automation, reliability, and recovery.

A [2013 IHOST announcement](https://www.prweb.com/releases/ihost_llc_partners_with_smarter_tools/prweb11157484.htm) documents my role as CEO and the company's expansion into turnkey cloud VPS services through HostedAppliance.net.

### Today — Engineering the systems around AI

My current focus is agentic software development and practical AI infrastructure.

I work on the capabilities around the models: durable context, custom agent runtimes, repeatable workflows, connections between tools, explicit permissions, and operations that can be inspected and resumed.

The same questions connect this work to my earlier career: Can someone depend on it? Can they understand what it is doing? Can they recover when something goes wrong?

## What I'm building now

- **[LocalSetup](https://github.com/CruxExperts/LocalSetup)** — A shared operating framework for AI development. Version-controlled project context, engineering standards, reusable skills, workflows, and adapters for tools including Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, and Oh My Pi.

- **[OMP OmniRoute Provider](https://github.com/CruxExperts/OMP-OmniRoute-Provider)** — Connects Oh My Pi to an existing OmniRoute gateway, with guided setup, live model discovery, and optional administration using separate management credentials and explicit confirmation.

- **[EnvMan](https://github.com/CruxExperts/EnvMan)** — Encrypted local environment management for terminals, scripts, and automation, with import previews, portable encrypted backups, and optional agent-skill integration.

- **[BestBackup / bbackup](https://github.com/CruxExperts/BestBackup)** — Encrypted Linux file backups, selected database workflows, verified file restores, and observable scheduled jobs, with a structured command interface for automation.

**LocalSetup is the foundation of this work.** It gives supported agents a lasting reference for how a project should be built, tested, and maintained. Its [LSCli runtime](https://github.com/CruxExperts/LocalSetup/blob/main/ls/docs/LSCLI.md) adds explicit execution profiles, controlled tool access, and session continuation and recovery.

These projects are independently useful and share an operational approach: make setup repeatable, preserve useful context, expose progress, and design for recovery. LocalSetup includes optional EnvMan integration; the other tools address complementary parts of development and operations.

I also build focused utilities such as **[Cinnamon Panel Anchor](https://github.com/CruxExperts/cinnamon-panel-anchor)**, which keeps Cinnamon panels attached to the intended monitor on Linux Mint with X11, even when display indexes change.

## How I approach engineering

- **Make knowledge durable.** Keep instructions, decisions, and useful context in files that can be reviewed and maintained.
- **Make work observable.** Provide clear progress, useful diagnostics, and enough evidence to understand an outcome.
- **Build for recovery.** Treat backups, restore checks, resumable work, and interrupted operations as part of the design.
- **Keep authority explicit.** Give tools the access they need, with deliberate boundaries around credentials and consequential actions.
- **Stay close to the business.** Understand the people, constraints, and responsibilities the system has to serve.
